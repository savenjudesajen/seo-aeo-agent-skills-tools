# The consumer / B2C anchor (when no review or analyst category exists)

The anchoring principle anchors a run on the third-party category surface where the brand has proof: an analyst
peer-review market for enterprise services, G2 or Capterra for self-serve SaaS. **Neither carries consumer
categories.** A pottery studio, a dog-walking service, a houseplant subscription: none sits in a market a buyer researches
through a review platform.

The wrong move is falling back to organic-overlap "competitor" tools. That is the failure the anchoring principle exists
to prevent, and it is worse on consumer terms: consumer head terms are enormous and generic, so overlap surfaces media
sites, comparison farms and unrelated apps.

Examples are invented, for **ClientN**, a ceramics studio (classes, open-studio memberships, a kiln-firing service for
home potters).

## The substitute: the engines' own answer surface

Read the pages the answer engines already resolve consumer questions to. Three sources, in order of value:

**1. The organic PAGES export per competitor, with any AI-prompt count the tool reports.** Some keyword tools now report,
per page, a count of observed AI prompts resolving to it. Sorting a competitor's pages by that count ranks what the
engines actually answer FROM: the consumer equivalent of a category page, built from the engines rather than a vendor
directory.

**2. All-keywords exports on the category head terms, filtered to prompt shape.** Keep a row if it is prompt-shaped (six
or more words, or opens with a question word, or ends with a question mark: the one definition used everywhere), above a
volume floor you state. That is where the buyer's phrasing lives at consumer scale.

**3. The client's own FAQ page, for product scope and eligibility.** It carries what the product does, who may use it,
and the client's vocabulary for both. Read it before sizing any line; it also feeds the eligibility pass
(`scope-and-fit.md`, product-fit pass 4).

**Check for exports you already hold before writing a single prompt.** List the downloads folder and the project's
research files first. A prompt universe drafted from the homepage feature list while earlier exports sit unopened ends
up targeting near-zero-demand terms and using the client's internal feature names. An unopened export is the cheapest
grounding there is.

## The two-vocabulary rule is the highest-value step on a consumer run

The gap between what the client calls a feature and what buyers type is largest on consumer products, which are
marketed with invented names. Invented illustration:

| the client's name | what buyers type | note |
|---|---|---|
| Kiln Share (firing for pieces made at home) | `where can i fire my pottery`, `pottery firing service` | buyer terms carry far more volume than the page's own phrase |
| Glaze Lab evenings (members mix their own glazes) | `pottery painting` | a DIFFERENT mechanism (painting shop-bought bisque is not glaze mixing): do not adopt it |
| Wheel Hour | `rent a pottery wheel by the hour` | the same mechanism under a plainer name: adopt the buyer's word |
| Clay Circle (open-studio membership) | `pottery studio membership` | |

**Check each product name against the keyword export before you use it in a prompt or a page title.** A page titled with a
low-volume house phrase while large demand sits one synonym away is the commonest consumer miss.

The second row shows the rule's limit: the buyer's high-volume word can describe a genuinely different mechanism from the
client's feature. Mapping to it would be wrong. Check that the buyer's word means the same thing before adopting it; if
it does not, you are in the next section.

## A zero-volume theme can be the best finding in the set

Where a client ships something no competitor offers, no vocabulary exists for it, so every tool reports zero. That is a
category with no name yet, and tracking a prompt for it is how you watch it open.

The payoff is often a factual correction rather than a content project: when engines answer the question that describes
the client's unique feature by naming rivals that do not offer it, an engine giving a wrong answer a competitor benefits
from is the cheapest fix on the roadmap, and it is a correction to pursue with the engines (and the sources they cite),
not a page to write.

## What carries over unchanged from the B2B flow

Everything downstream: the seed chain, the recirculation loop, the product-fit gate (including the eligibility and
liability pass, which regulated or restricted consumer brands fail most often), the data traps, the grounding hierarchy,
the stage test, the open-goal sweep. Only the ANCHOR changes. Say in the deliverable which anchor was used and why: a
reader who knows the method will look for the review or analyst surface and needs to see that its absence was handled,
not skipped.
