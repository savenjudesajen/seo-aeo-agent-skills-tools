# Cross-industry benchmark: 12 industries sections, page by page

The starter set behind `data/benchmark.py`: one overview page and one industry page (financial services, the
industry nearly every company covers) for 12 well-known companies outside any single client's category (cloud, enterprise software, consulting and IT services). Each entry
paraphrases what the page does, in page order, and what is worth copying. Nothing here is page copy: re-read the
live page before you quote it, and expect pages to have moved (several overview addresses in this set redirect or
sit inside broader pages).

How to use it: open the leaderboard to see which sites had the best ideas, then the entry for the page you need,
then the live page. Type ids refer to `reference/section-types.md`.

## Shapes seen across the set
- **Three overview shapes.** A directory (an A to Z list or a dense text list with one line per industry), a card
  grid with a one-line value statement per card, and a research-first page where industries are tabs of a research
  library. A fourth case: no standalone overview at all, with the industry list as one section of a broader page.
- **The overview pages are short.** Most run a few hundred words: hero, industry list or cards, a proof or research
  row, next steps. The depth sits on the industry pages.
- **The industry pages share a stack.** Hero with an industry H1 and a button, a local menu, the industry's
  challenge or value pillars, segments, priorities or use cases, solution mapping, proof (stories, metrics, quotes),
  research cards, sometimes regulations, a trust block and an FAQ, then a closing band.
- **What almost nobody shows.** A named reviewer, a page date, figures with sources on the overview, an FAQ on the
  overview, and structured data beyond breadcrumbs.

## AWS (cloud)
- **Overview page** (`aws.amazon.com/industries`): hero with one button to sales; an approach section with two scale
  numbers (pre-built solutions, industry partners) and a short video; one paragraph of named industry firsts across
  sectors, with no logo wall; a card grid where every card carries a one or two sentence value statement (the
  financial services card names its four segments) with a show-all control; a blog promo; three next-step cards by
  buyer type (sign up, sales, partner).
- **Industry page**: sticky local menu; hero with a contact button; an intro with a scope claim; a segments row
  with one page per segment; story cards with the outcome in the headline; a current-year sector report offered as
  an open PDF; an executive customer spotlight video; four benefit statements; an AI feature; a gated e-book; named
  partners with one line each; marketplace listings; a closing button.
- **Copy**: scale numbers early, the industry-firsts paragraph, value lines on every card, segments with their own
  pages, an ungated dated report.

## Microsoft (software)
- **Overview page** (an industry carousel on a broader AI page): event
  banner; hero with two buttons; an ask box with two suggested questions; product carousel by job; trust block;
  industry carousel where three cards list their segments as bullets; a tabbed news, insights and events module;
  story cards with a number in the headline; a ten-question FAQ with definition-first answers; next steps by role.
- **Industry page**: campaign banner; hero with a video; gated e-book; four value pillars; segment cards with a
  one-line promise each; a security and compliance block; a story carousel where each card carries a quote, the
  speaker's title, one or two results and the products used; partners mapped to industry jobs; a product carousel; a
  full-width band on one named regulation; typed resource cards; next steps that route to a trust portal and
  documentation, not only to sales; a compliance footnote.
- **Copy**: the story card anatomy, the single-regulation band, partners as jobs, next steps that include evidence.

## IBM (software with a consulting arm)
- **Overview page**: a site banner for its newest flagship report with one headline figure; hero with a button and a
  jump link to next steps; an intro and a large card grid with a one-line promise per card (financial services split
  across three cards); customer cards with a number in the outcome line; a research carousel where every card is
  labelled by format and one card is an analyst recognition; next steps (contact, newsletter, services, innovation
  studio).
- **Industry page**: webinar banner; hero with a button; an overview that names the industry's pressures in buyer
  terms plus a customer video; business priorities, each a problem paragraph paired with the one asset that answers
  it; logo-tab case studies with one or two big numbers each; product and service cards; a research carousel that
  includes an ROI calculator; an industry event block; a closing button.
- **Copy**: priority-to-asset pairing, typed research cards, the calculator as a card.

## Oracle (software)
- **Overview page**: jump links; hero paragraph that mentions regulatory needs; flagship event promo with city and
  dates; a seven-line why block; industry playbooks framed as planning standards across all its industries; a
  silent logo row; an A to Z list of about twenty industries with no descriptions; physical industry lab sites.
- **Industry page**: breadcrumb; hero with an expert-contact button; a spotlight row mixing a thought-leadership
  e-book, a market headline, a dated analyst ranking and a press release; line-of-business tabs, each with
  sub-solutions in plain definitional sentences and named standards; a full product index by solution and sector; a
  logo carousel. No closing band.
- **Copy**: the spotlight row, definitional sub-solution text, the product index for large portfolios.

## SAP (software)
- **Overview page**: hero with jump links straight to the industry list; seven cross-industry process cards, each
  with a one-line outcome, as a second way in; an A to Z list of over twenty industries; a page feedback prompt.
- **Industry page**: hero whose line under the H1 names the four buyer types served, with a demo button and a jump
  link; an event banner; a value block with outcomes mapped to products; solution panels whose cards are labelled as
  the company's own or a partner's, with named standards; customer logos linking a filtered story finder; an
  industry-only event; segment-specific research cards; a get-started block with public product road maps per
  segment; a seven-question FAQ with one or two sentence answers linking trust evidence; feedback prompt.
- **Copy**: buyer types in the hero line, the FAQ, road maps per segment, own vs partner labels.

## Adobe (software)
- **Overview page** (`business.adobe.com/industries/...`): hero with no button; a
  card grid with a specific one-sentence value statement per industry; a closing button. The thinnest page in the
  set.
- **Industry page**: an industry sub-menu that turns the industry into a mini-site of use-case pages; hero; a strip
  of six customer logos, each with one bold metric, across the industry's segments; four capability tabs, each with
  three outcomes and a link to its use-case page; three adoption figures, each with a named third-party source, one
  dated; a named customer quote; typed resource cards; a closing button.
- **Copy**: sourced figures (the most citable format in the set), one metric per logo, hub-and-spoke use-case pages.

## Deloitte (consulting)
- **Overview page**: hero; a coverage statement that gives the number of industries and sectors, then six cards
  whose sentences name each industry's sectors; cross-links to services and alliances.
- **Industry page**: quick links including a request-for-proposal shortcut; hero with a subscribe button; sector
  links; a challenge statement that names the forces reshaping the industry; sector cards; problem-first story
  headlines with numbers in the teasers; a year-labelled outlook for every sector plus annual predictions; a named
  method band; capability cards; sector leaders with title, legal entity and direct contact routes.
- **Copy**: the coverage number, the challenge statement, year-labelled outlooks, named leaders.

## Accenture (consulting)

  capabilities list; a partner count with logos; an industries list where every entry opens with a challenge line in
  that industry's terms; a stories teaser; analyst recognition with year, report and axis result.
- **Industry page**: local menu; hero; a four-figure state-of-the-industry strip; agenda cards linking deeper

  industry-specific analyst recognition with firm, report, year, placement and field size; regional practice

- **Copy**: challenge lines per industry, industry-specific recognition, four industry figures at the top of the
  page.

## McKinsey (consulting)
- **Overview page**: an interactive hero; a directory where each practice has one plain
  sentence on whom it serves; featured cards (stories, alliances, solutions); links to firm history and client
  service policies; contact.
- **Industry page**: short hero; a sub-practice and capability menu; a promo band; named benchmarking and data
  products with plain definitions; one dated featured interview; one dated featured case study whose headline states
  the lesson; a practice contact.
- **Copy**: plain scope sentences, dated content cards, a governance link on the overview.

## PwC (consulting)
- **Overview page**: a research-first page: hero, a subscribe prompt, a research library with one tab per industry
  where every card shows its format and read time, stories tagged by industry, industry cards with one sentence each,
  a newsletter.
- **Industry page**: a point of view in the first line above the H1; an index of six anchor links; a featured thesis
  with a named framework; an overview; sector cards that each state a current view, not a service list; research
  cards with the survey year and sample sizes; stories naming the platform used; a newsletter; named leaders with
  contact routes.
- **Copy**: the index, opinionated sector cards, samples on research cards, format and read time on cards.

## Capgemini (consulting)
- **Overview page**: a sub-navigation bar with the page trail, a menu of every industry and a contact button; a hero
  with the H1; a short intro on why industry knowledge matters; a grid of about fifteen industry cards, each an
  image and a name with no description line.
- **Industry page** (banking and capital markets): the same sub-navigation bar; the H1, then a heading and paragraph
  naming the pressures banks face, with a scale claim about the share of large banks it serves; a row of promoted
  reports and trend pieces, two of them year-labelled; segment cards, each with a paragraph and its own page; a
  contact block; featured services for the industry; research cards labelled by kind (point of view, research,
  report); story cards, one with a result in the headline; dated expert articles with authors; expert profiles with
  titles and a contact route; recognition cards with firm and assessment; a partner logo row; a closing link to
  follow the company's social page.
- **Copy**: research cards labelled by kind, dated expert articles, segment pages with a paragraph each, recognition
  tied to the industry.

## Cognizant (IT services)
- **Overview page**: hero with a promise line and no button; a section menu with anchors to the page's parts; a
  short overview; a grid of about eighteen industry cards, each with a one-line description and a link; a closing
  contact form.
- **Industry page** (banking): breadcrumb and a sticky local menu (overview, segments, featured work, recognition,
  latest thinking, leadership, contact); hero with a promo for a related article; an overview with a point of view
  on the industry and its own platforms; three segment cards with a line each; a carousel of about a dozen story
  cards, several with a number in the headline; a recognition carousel naming the analyst firm and assessment; a
  carousel of resource cards (reports and posts); one named industry leader with title; a closing contact form.
- **Copy**: the sticky local menu that mirrors the page's sections, story headlines that lead with the outcome.
