# CRO stage model and default section template

Sequence every section (the kept ones and the new ones) with these six stages. The logic: someone
weighing a purchase asks a predictable series of questions ("is this for me?", "do others trust it?",
"what is it, exactly?", "what do I get?", "what could go wrong?", "what do I do now?"), so the page answers
them in that order. Adapt the sections per page; keep the stage order, which is the conversion spine.

## The six conversion stages

1. **Capture (above the fold).** In a few seconds the visitor knows who the page is for, what outcome it
   promises and what to do next. A hero that names the audience and the outcome, one leading action and
   a single trust cue. On long pages, a jump-link bar keeps that action in reach.

2. **Build credibility.** Show that people like them already chose this: customer logos, a user count or
   an average rating, and a short band of outcome numbers, each with a source.

3. **Orient.** Answer "what is it, exactly?" before any detail: a three-step "how it works", or a short
   "who it's for / what you get" block.

4. **Educate.** Benefits, strongest first: open with the one competitors can't match, then the rest.
   Push specs and fine print into tabs or accordions so the page stays scannable.

5. **De-risk.** Clear the reasons not to act while interest is high: use cases or "this is for you if…"
   so visitors self-identify; compatibility ("works with what you already have"); then trust and safety
   (privacy, guarantees, credentials, security). When the buyer's main worry is risk, move this stage up
   rather than leaving it near the footer.

6. **Prove and convert.** Customer stories with outcomes and a quote; recognition (review-site badges,
   awards, press, or analyst reports in categories that have them); why choose this over the alternatives;
   pricing or how to buy; an FAQ for last-minute doubts; a soft path for visitors who aren't ready (a guide,
   a newsletter, a short demo video); and a closing call to action so the page never dead-ends.

## Default section template (page order, any considered purchase)

A starting point, not a mandate: keep, rename or drop per page. Mark each row with its status (Keep /
Rework / Move / Promote / New, defined below), and name the competitor(s) the pattern is drawn from.

| # | Section | Stage |
|---|---------|-------|
| 1 | Hero: who it's for + outcome + leading action + a single trust cue | Capture |
| 2 | Jump-link bar with the action pinned (long pages only) | Capture |
| 3 | Social proof strip (logos, user count or rating) | Credibility |
| 4 | Outcomes band (numbers with sources) | Credibility |
| 5 | How it works (three steps) or who it's for | Orient |
| 6 | Strongest benefit | Educate |
| 7–8 | Other benefits, one block each | Educate |
| 9 | Use cases / "for you if…" | De-risk |
| 10 | Compatibility or integrations | De-risk |
| 11 | Trust and safety (privacy, guarantees, credentials) | De-risk |
| 12 | Customer stories + quote | Prove |
| 13 | Recognition (review badges, awards, press) | Prove |
| 14 | Why us vs the alternatives (optionally a comparison table) | Prove |
| 15 | Pricing or plans | Convert |
| 16 | FAQ | Convert |
| 17 | Soft path (guide, newsletter or demo video) | Convert |
| 18 | Closing call to action | Convert |
| 19 | Footer | — |

### How the template bends by page type

- **Software or app page:** Orient is a three-step "how it works"; Educate can split benefits by role;
  Prove often carries a free trial or demo as the leading action and review-site badges as recognition.
- **Services or agency page:** Orient becomes "what we do, for whom, how working together runs"; Educate
  is the service lines; De-risk is process, team credentials and price ranges; Prove is case studies with
  outcomes.
- **E-commerce product page:** Capture is product media, price, the key option choice and add-to-cart;
  Credibility is the rating summary beside the title; Educate is benefits, then specs; De-risk is
  delivery cost and timing, returns and guarantees right next to the buy button, plus size or fit guidance
  in context; Prove is the full reviews module. Show stock status clearly and avoid costs that appear only
  later in checkout.
- **Local business page:** Capture adds location and opening hours to the hero; De-risk is licences,
  insurance and service area; Prove is local reviews and a map; Convert is call, book or directions, with
  the phone number tappable on mobile.

## Attribution and prevalence method

- **Prevalence** = the number of competitors (of N) whose page contains that section type.
- **Drawn from** = the 1–3 competitors whose execution is the best example. This is what you screenshot as
  proof for a New section.
- **Status on the target page** = Keep (unchanged) / Rework (same slot, new content) / Move (another
  slot) / Promote (raise its order or prominence) / New (missing: add). The same set is used in SKILL.md
  and in the deliverable builder.
- Prevalence ranks the gaps; fit with the positioning can override it (see SKILL.md Step 4). Say so in the
  rationale when it does.

## Section types to look for when de-duplicating

Hero · jump links · logo strip · ratings or user counts · outcome numbers · how it works · who it's for ·
benefit grid · benefit deep-dives · demo video or interactive tour · use cases · compatibility or
integrations · privacy, security or guarantees · credentials and certifications · customer stories ·
quotes · review-site badges (G2, Capterra) · awards and press · comparison with alternatives · pricing
or plans · FAQ · guides and resources · newsletter sign-up · closing call to action · footer.
