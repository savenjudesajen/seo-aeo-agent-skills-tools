# Report structure: two views, one engine

The same run produces both views. The external view persuades; the internal view prepares the call.

## External scorecard (prospect-facing landing page): 9 sections
Built to be understood by a **non-technical owner**.
1. **Hero:** composite score, status band and the account's hook (AI recommends [Competitor] for
   the buyer's questions, not [Company]), in your own words. Add one plain sentence explaining what
   the page is, e.g. "We asked [the engines you probed] the questions your buyers ask and checked
   whether they mention you." Don't skip this line; without it a non-expert is lost from the first
   scroll.
2. **Stat cards (3):** three scannable, specific numbers. A strong trio: the named count ("1 in N"),
   the competitor multiple ("[Competitor] is named K× more"), and the contrast ("N pages, almost none
   used by AI").
3. **Scoring breakdown + radar:** 7 pillars against the target of 70, with plain labels. The point:
   the gap is specific and diagnosable, not luck.
4. **Competitor leaderboard:** who AI names instead, with the prospect placed in the ranking.
5. **The receipts:** the real questions asked, whether they were named, who was named instead. This
   is the evidence that makes the rest hard to argue with.
6. **The diagnosis, gated:** first paragraph visible; the rest blurred behind a button that offers
   the full plan (label it in the brand's voice). This creates the open loop.
7. **The plays (3):** the fixes, ordered by impact. They make the bad news solvable.
8. **Proof / case studies:** social proof. **Hide this section entirely until you have real
   results.** Never ship placeholder boxes to a prospect.
9. **Final call to action:** the booking ask.

## Internal view (team-only, ungated)
Keeps the hero, stats, scoring, leaderboard and receipts; drops the sales furniture; unlocks the
brief; and adds:
- **Full brief + a 90-day and 180-day plan.**
- **Where the funnel breaks:** page mix by funnel stage. Often the finding is not a missing stage
  but that every stage earns zero citations.
- **Folder breakdown:** content volume against citation yield per folder. "N blog posts, 0
  citations" is the most actionable line you can hand a marketer.
- **Content gaps:** page types the winners have and the prospect doesn't.
- **Where AI gets its answers:** the third-party sources engines read for this category, biggest
  blind spots first. That's where to earn mentions.
- **SERP competitors:** authority and keyword overlap, tying the AI picture back to classic SEO.
- **Top recommendations:** ranked, each written as **The gap / The business cost / The fix**. Item 1
  is what you lead the call with.

## Rules
- Anything you can't evidence, mark pending; don't invent it. "Pending continuous tracking" is
  credible; a fabricated number is fatal.
- State the sample honestly: a snapshot of N prompts on M engines, not full tracking. Note any engine
  that failed to run.
- Internal diagnostics depend on deeper crawl data and can legitimately be thin. Say "no data for
  this run"; never imply the prospect has no footprint.
