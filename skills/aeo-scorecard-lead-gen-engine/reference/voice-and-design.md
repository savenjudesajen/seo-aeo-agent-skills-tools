# Voice and design

## Voice: receipts first, no fluff
The scorecard works best as a question the prospect has to answer, not a verdict you have to defend.
- **Lead with the specific, not the adjective.** Replace a judgement ("visibility is poor") with the
  observed fact: which buyer question was asked and which brands the answer named instead.
- **When the prospect pushes back, give before you ask.** Acknowledge what is fair in the objection,
  add one insight they didn't have, then ask your question. Pushback means they are paying attention.
- **Anchor on absence, not the percentage.** A number is arguable; being missing from a specific
  buyer question is not.
- **Concede the sample, stress the direction.** A snapshot can be off; admit it, then point out that
  even a generous margin leaves the gap in place while more buying research moves to AI answers.
- The question underneath everything: should your brand be the answer in your own category? If yes,
  there's a gap. If no, who should be, and why isn't it you?
- No hedging and no filler openers. If a sentence doesn't carry a fact or move the argument, cut it.
  Follow the brand's own writing guidelines, if it has them; otherwise check each paragraph for one
  concrete fact, one claim and no stock phrases before it ships.

## Plain English is required
**The reader is an owner or principal, not an SEO.** Translate every technical pillar name on the
prospect-facing page; keep technical vocabulary for the internal view. If a smart non-expert on your
own team has to work to understand the page, the buyer won't bother. Their confusion is data, not a
knowledge gap to excuse.

Write the plain labels in the client's market language and test them on a non-expert. A starting set
(change the wording to suit the brand):

| Internal (technical) | Prospect-facing (plain) | Short radar label |
|---|---|---|
| Citation share | How often AI recommends you | Recommended |
| Content structure | Can AI read your site easily | Readable |
| Answer readiness | Do your pages give direct answers | Direct answers |
| Competitor gaps | Questions competitors own | Competitor gaps |
| Freshness | How current your content is | Up to date |
| Entity clarity | Is your brand one clear identity | One identity |
| Off-site mentions | Who else talks about you | Outside mentions |

Other swaps: *cited* → **recommended** · *citation leaderboard* → **brands AI recommends instead** ·
*probes / prompts* → **the questions we asked** · *Not cited / Cited* → **Missing / Recommended** · a
low status band → a neutral label such as **Room to grow**, never a ranking word.

## Design
Use the client's own design system and template if they have one: pull palette, type and layout
from their design file. If you only have view access, approximate and say that you did.

Neutral default when there is no client system:
- one background surface and one accent colour, used for calls to action, scores and highlights;
- a separate warning colour used only for the bad numbers;
- score colours by band, so the eye finds the problem before the brain reads it;
- one readable display face for headlines and one for body text;
- a long single-column scroll with generous spacing and no clever navigation (the page has one job);
- the radar carries the "diagnosable, not luck" argument: short axis labels on the chart, a plain
  sentence per pillar in the list beside it.
