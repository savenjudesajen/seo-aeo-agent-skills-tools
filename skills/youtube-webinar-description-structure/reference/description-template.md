# Description template: 10 blocks, in this order

Target length 1,500 to 3,000 characters (the benchmark median is about 1,650; long-form webinars on the strongest
channels run to about 3,000). Hard ceiling 5,000 (YouTube's limit). Plain text, a blank line between blocks.

| # | Block | What goes in it | Why (benchmark) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | One or two sentences, 150 characters or less. Head term in the first sentence, plus the concrete outcome or tension. A number, named standard or customer name if the transcript has one | Shown in search and above the fold. The median first line on the top channels is about 170 characters |
| 2 | **Primary link** | One line: `Learn more about <product or topic> -> <URL with UTMs>` | IBM, Gartner, Semrush and Google Cloud all place one link here |
| 3 | **Answer paragraph** | 40 to 70 words that directly answer prompt 1 in plain language, self-contained, quotable by an AI engine. Names, numbers, standards | The Ahrefs and IBM prose style, made short; the passage answer engines lift |
| 4 | **Context** | 2 to 3 sentences: who speaks (name, title, company), the series or episode, the customer, what is demoed | Gartner and Microsoft Developer show pages |
| 5 | **What you'll learn** | Localized heading, then 4 to 6 lines starting with "- ", each a concrete takeaway from the transcript in the words buyers search | Microsoft, Gartner, Semrush, Google Cloud |
| 6 | **Chapters** | Localized heading, then 6 to 15 timestamps from the transcript. Omit the block when there is no transcript | About half of long-form top videos carry them |
| 7 | **Speakers** | One line per speaker: Name, Title, Company | Microsoft, Google Cloud |
| 8 | **Resources** | Replay or landing page (only if live), related webinar or playlist, docs or blog. Each line: label -> URL | Resource-link blocks on Microsoft and Gartner pages |
| 9 | **Subscribe** | One line with the channel's subscribe link | About a quarter of top videos; cheap |
| 10 | **Hashtags** | 3 to 5 on the very last line. The first 3 show above the title, so lead with the topic tags | About 6 in 10 top videos |

Optional after block 7: `Products mentioned:` one line naming the products shown (a Google Cloud Tech pattern;
an entity signal for AI engines).

UTMs on the primary link: `utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<series or video>`, or the client's
own convention if they have one.

## Headings in other languages

Blocks 5 to 8 carry a short heading ("What you'll learn:", "Chapters:", "Speakers:", "Resources:"). For a video
in another language, write those four headings and the whole description in that language, using the
punctuation conventions of that language, and reuse the same four headings on every row in that language. For
example, in Spanish: "Lo que aprenderás:", "Capítulos:", "Ponentes:", "Recursos:". Have a native speaker check the
headings once if the client publishes in that language regularly.

## Worked example (English, transcript-backed, fictional company)

Trussline is a fictional construction-scheduling software company; every name, URL and number below is
invented. Head term: "look-ahead schedule". Prompt 1: "how do general contractors build a three week look ahead
schedule from the master schedule".

Title: `Look-Ahead Schedule Demo: Plan 3 Weeks of Site Work | Trussline` (63 characters; "Webinar" in the suffix would push it to 71, so the suffix is shortened before the head term is cut)

```
A look-ahead schedule turns the master plan into the next three weeks of real crew work. Watch a general contractor build one live in 30 minutes.

Learn more about Trussline Look-Ahead Planner -> https://www.trussline.example/look-ahead?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=webinar_replay

A three-week look-ahead schedule breaks the master schedule into daily tasks for each trade, checks every task for open constraints such as missing drawings, materials or inspections, and is updated in a weekly coordination meeting. Teams that clear constraints before work starts lose fewer days to idle crews and rework.

In episode 2 of the Site Planning webinar series, Jordan Sample, Senior Solutions Engineer at Trussline, builds a look-ahead schedule for a four-storey office project and runs a constraint review with the site team.

What you'll learn:
- How to pull three weeks of tasks from a master schedule without retyping them
- Which constraints to check before a task is marked ready
- How to run a weekly look-ahead meeting with every trade in 30 minutes
- How to track percent plan complete and use it to spot slipping trades

Chapters:
00:00 Why master schedules miss the next three weeks
02:15 Pulling look-ahead tasks from the master schedule
06:40 Breaking activities into daily crew tasks
11:05 Logging constraints: drawings, materials, inspections
16:30 Running the weekly look-ahead meeting
21:10 Tracking percent plan complete by trade
25:45 Sharing the schedule with subcontractors on mobile
28:20 Q&A: scheduling around weather delays

Speakers:
Jordan Sample, Senior Solutions Engineer, Trussline

Resources:
Site Planning webinar series -> https://www.trussline.example/webinars/site-planning
Watch episode 1, master schedule basics -> https://www.trussline.example/webinars/master-schedule
Look-ahead schedule template -> https://www.trussline.example/templates/look-ahead

Subscribe for more construction planning webinars -> <channel URL>?sub_confirmation=1

#LookAheadSchedule #ConstructionScheduling #Trussline
```

What makes it pass: head term in the first sentence of the hook and in the title's first 45 characters, one
link in line 2, an answer paragraph that stands on its own, four transcript-backed bullets, eight ascending
chapters from 00:00 with the Q&A named by its main question, hashtags alone on the last line, no dashes.
