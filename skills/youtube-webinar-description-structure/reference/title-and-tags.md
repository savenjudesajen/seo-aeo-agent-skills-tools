# Titles, tags, hashtags, playlists

## Title (70 characters max; the first ~50 are what mobile and search show)

1. **Lead with the head term** in the wording buyers search (from the keyword pass, not the webinar's internal
   name).
2. **Match the SERP format** for that term: definition ("What Is X?"), how-to ("How to X"), guide or explainer
   ("X: <angle>"), demo ("X Demo: <what is shown>"), customer story ("How <Customer> <does X>").
3. **Suffix** with the series or brand after a pipe when it fits: `| <Brand> Webinar` in English; in another
   language, `| <the word for webinar in that language> <Brand>` in that language's usual word order. Drop the
   suffix before you cut the head term.
4. **No prompt as title.** No question title except "What Is X?" on a definition SERP. No clickbait, no
   all-caps words, no segment the video doesn't cover. The gate accepts English and Spanish "What is" openers;
   for another language, pass its wording with `--definition-opener`.
5. Patterns the top channels use: keyword first, then the benefit ("What Is X? <what the viewer gets>"), "X: Y"
   explainers, and the series name after a pipe ("<topic> | <show name>").

## Tags (metadata field)

8 to 15 tags, 500 characters total max. Order: head term, secondary terms, product names, brand, format
("webinar", "demo"), language variants only if the video is bilingual. The benchmark median is about 5, but the
strongest SEO channels use 11 to 15 specific tags.

## Hashtags (end of description)

3 to 5, alone on the last line. The first three show above the title: two topic tags, then the brand tag.
CamelCase, no spaces, no dashes.

## Playlists

One series playlist per recurring format (e.g. "<Series name> Webinar Series", "<Product> Tips") plus one
topic playlist. Playlists rank on their own in YouTube search, so name them with the head term.
