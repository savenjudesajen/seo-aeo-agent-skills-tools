# Public benchmark: comparison hubs in other industries (5 hubs)

Research notes, paraphrased (no page copy), on comparison hubs outside SaaS: three vendors and two software
marketplaces. Together with the SaaS notes they form the reusable cross-industry set. Add three to five
review-publisher "best of" pages on each run (see `benchmark-method.md`): the publisher patterns in
`section-types.md` (verdicts, bylines, change logs, also-tested lists) are not carried by these hubs. Pages change:
re-read before you cite or capture.

## G2 compare hub (software marketplace)
- Hub: /compare, about 450 words.
- Sections: hero describing the three ways it helps (alternatives, side-by-side, advisors) with a verified-review
  scale figure; an open "X vs Y" picker; popular category entry points; a trending-products list and an
  "alternatives to trending software" list, each with a one-sentence note on how the list is built; most-viewed
  head-to-head pairs shown as two logos with "VS" and a one-line definition of what a comparison page scores; an
  advisor chat.
- Worth copying: the open picker; a "how this list is built" sentence on every algorithmic list (a light
  methodology statement answer engines can quote); pair tiles as the visual pattern for a vs-page directory.

## Capterra compare hub (software marketplace)
- Hub: /compare/, about 1,200 words. No date and no byline.
- Sections: hero with a two-to-four product comparison builder; featured products with a paid-placement label
  written into the block that separates paid listings from ranked lists; popular pair cards showing rating and review
  count, price, a features score and an ease-of-use score for both sides; an advisor call offer; a short explainer
  with a sourced survey statistic on the cost of a wrong purchase; a glossary of every comparison dimension with
  annotated screenshots; three buyer tips; a trust trio (independent method, how reviews are verified, how the site is
  paid); an email capture. The advisor block and the email form exist only in the JavaScript-rendered page. The
  featured set rotated between reads, and every popular pair contained a featured product.
- Worth copying: the paid-placement label inside the block; pair cards with a mini verdict (works as a vendor's
  "<Client> vs <Rival>" tiles); a dimension glossary that doubles as the method; the three-part trust footer.

## Shopify (ecommerce platform)
- Hub: /compare, about 800 words. Localised in many languages. No structured data found.
- Sections: hero with one button; a band of four headline stats (no sources on the page); a rival directory split
  into featured cards, single-platform comparisons and three-way comparisons, each card tagged with the segment it
  serves; a migration reassurance right after the list; a capability tour in four groups with a few figures; a link to
  a self-benchmark tool that reports on the reader's current site; an email offer; short customer quotes and a logo
  wall; a pricing snapshot with a starting price; help paths (community, support, partners) and a closing offer.
- Worth copying: featured, single and three-way splits with segment tags; switching reassurance after the list; a
  self-benchmark tool. Copy the stat band only with a source on every number.

## Ahrefs (SEO software)
- The /vs path serves a single head-to-head against its main rival (an older multi-rival hub appears retired; a
  second vs URL returns 404).
- Sections: hero with a direct "how is it better" claim; a logo wall with an adoption figure; a paired data band
  (each metric shown for both vendors, including one where the rival's raw number is larger, with the definitional
  difference spelled out); an external crawler-activity ranking table with an "as of" date and a link to the source;
  a numbered list of exclusive features, each with a screenshot; a short comparison table that mixes factual rows with
  joke rows; an industry-context section contrasting the rival's change of ownership with its own independence and
  linking a changelog; a closing band with a live sign-up counter and a downloadable comparison brochure.
- Worth copying: the paired data band with honest definitions; the dated external ranking; the ownership context;
  the brochure for internal buy-in. Do not copy the joke rows: answer engines lift table cells literally.

## Semrush (SEO software)
- Hub: /vs/, under 200 words of main content. CollectionPage structured data.
- Sections: hero with an all-in-one frame; a customer logo strip; two rival cards, each with three differentiator
  bullets and a full-comparison link; third-party ratings from three review sites in one row (no counts or dates); a
  closing band.
- Worth copying: per-rival cards with three reasons each, a compact pattern for a hub with few rivals. Add counts and
  dates to the ratings row.

## Sections vendor hubs usually lack (and how a vendor adapts each)
1. **An answer-first verdict.** Open with two or three sentences on which option fits which job, including when a
   rival is the better choice.
2. **"Best for" labels on every product.** Give each rival card the job it genuinely does best, and the client one
   too; "best X for Y" is how buyers prompt.
3. **A comparison table with a stated basis.** A factual attribute table built from rivals' public documentation,
   with a checked-on date; third-party ratings with review counts instead of self-awarded scores.
4. **A method with criteria.** A "How we compare" section: criteria, sources (rivals' own pages, analyst notes the
   client may quote, review sites), who did the work and when; publish any weights so buyers can re-weight.
5. **A named author plus a reviewer, with bios.** An in-house expert and a second named reviewer.
6. **A visible last-reviewed date and a change log.** On the hub and every vs page, with a short log of what changed.
7. **A disclosure of commercial interest.** Say plainly that the client wrote the page and sells one of the products,
   how rival facts were sourced, and how to request a correction.
8. **Strengths and limits for every product, the client's own included.** "Where <Rival> leads" and "Where <Client>
   leads", sourced, plus one honest limit.
9. **An evidence layer.** Footnote every number with its source and date and link public third-party data; drop
   claims that cannot be sourced.
10. **A use-case or segment directory.** "Compare by need" entry points that route to the relevant vs pages.
11. **A comparison selector.** A "compare up to three" picker over the same public attribute set, defaulting to the
    most-searched pairs (once the hub lists more rivals than one table holds).
12. **An also-considered list with reasons.** Adjacent tools buyers confuse with the client, one line each on why
    they solve a different problem.
13. **A buying guide or RFP checklist.** The questions a buyer should ask every vendor, the client included, as HTML
    text with an ungated download. Put the criteria before any claim, and answer the objections buyers raise by
    name; rivals left unnamed give answer engines nothing to attach a claim to.
14. **A prompt-shaped FAQ with FAQPage markup.** "Is <Client> an alternative to <Rival>?" and category-difference
    questions answered in two or three neutral sentences.
15. **Jump links or a sticky table of contents** with stable anchors for each section.

Also worth a look: an effort stat strip, a downloadable comparison brochure, per-product "last tested" dates, AI
chat greetings that change with the page, and, where the product has something measurable, a live table that shows
every provider's result for the reader's own inputs (proof in place of claims).
