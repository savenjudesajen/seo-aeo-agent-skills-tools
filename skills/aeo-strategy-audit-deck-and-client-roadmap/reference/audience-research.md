# Audience research: a required section of every AEO audit

Every audit deck carries an audience research section. It answers three questions the rest of the deck
depends on: who the buyers actually are, which search and AI engines they use, and which sites, channels
and communities they trust. Without it the AI-visibility section measures engines the audience may not
use, and the off-site plays pick channels by guesswork.

## Getting the data

Use an audience-research tool (e.g. SparkToro), in its web UI or from exports someone on the account
already pulled (check the client folder before pulling again).
- Export the CSVs where offered and keep the overview PDF. Values read off a chart are approximate: mark
  them with `~`.

## What to pull (two audiences, minimum)

1. **The client's website audience:** "people who visit <client domain>" (in the client's main market).
2. **The professional or buyer segment:** described the way the tool lets you, e.g. "people who talk about
   <category> and follow <trade publication>". This is the comparison base for over- and under-index.
Optional third: a competitor's website audience, when the client asks who a rival reaches.

From each report capture: demographics (gender, age range, job titles, industries), **search and AI tools**
used vs the national average, **social networks** used vs the average, **websites** visited (affinity,
authority, monthly visits, low-competition gems), **YouTube channels**, **podcasts**, **subreddits**,
**highest-affinity keywords** and **topics**.

## Required slides (long deck)

Open the analysis part of the deck with these:
1. **How the data is gathered:** one slide on what the data is (for example anonymised clickstream, search
   results, public social profiles) and what it covers (behaviours, characteristics, demographics).
2. **Demographics:** gender and age distribution, each with a segment read (which segment buys, which
   researches, which influences the decision). Label the base (e.g. "web visitors, <market>").
3. **Social networks vs the national average:** over- and under-index platforms, each with the concrete
   opportunity (named communities, professional-network themes, video formats).
4. **Comparison with the target personas or ICP:** map the data to the client's personas; call out any
   missing persona the data reveals.
5. **What the audience reads, watches and searches:** video channels, podcasts, highest-affinity keywords
   and topics, other websites visited (ask: are any of these direct competitors?), communities. Each list
   carries its action (content calendar, outreach, community play).
6. **Search and AI platforms:** which engines this audience uses more or less than average, the priority
   discovery channels, and, where usage is below average, the likely reason (regulated industries,
   institutional workflows and so on).

## How it feeds the rest of the audit

- **AI-visibility engine set:** track every engine the audience over-indexes on. If a professional segment
  uses a particular assistant at several times the national rate, that assistant joins the scorecard even
  if it is not one of the default engines.
- **Off-site plays** (Phase 4): the trade sites, podcasts, video channels and communities on these slides
  are the outreach and community targets. Cross-check them against the citation-source analysis: a site
  that is both high-affinity and cited by AI is a top-priority placement.
- **Prompt set** (Phase 3): highest-affinity keywords and topics seed prompts alongside Search Console and
  the keyword gap; job titles and personas set the persona column.
- **Measurement:** the audience's engine mix decides which engines the monthly scorecard reports.

## Condensed deck

One slide, "Who the buyer is and where they research": a demographics line, the search and AI engine
over/under-index chart, the top 6 to 8 sites, and three implications for AEO.

## Rules

- Every figure names its report and pull date in the speaker notes; chart-read values carry `~`.
- Keep tool names out of slide body copy (`writing-and-voice.md`); the source goes in the notes or the
  method slide's footer.
- If a chart's icons or labels cannot be identified, leave that figure out and raise it in chat; never
  guess which engine a bar is.
