# Worked examples (fictional)

Invented scenarios that show how the fit test decides and which template to send. Every brand, site and
page here is made up. The brand in every example is Brewnook (example.com), an online shop for home coffee
equipment with a large section of brewing guides. Its competitor list covers every other retailer of home
coffee gear, plus coffee subscriptions that also sell grinders and brewers. Quality floors are the defaults
in SKILL.md.

## Morning Grind Notes, a 3-way offer from a blogger with sister sites

- Ask: the owner of a coffee-hobby blog, morninggrind.example, also runs two sister sites (one on tea, one
  on kitchen gadgets). They offer a link to Brewnook from "one of the sister sites" if Brewnook links to
  their blog. They name their own blog but neither the sister site nor any page.
- Checks: Who: a publisher (pass). Want: a link from one of our pages (unknown until a page is named).
  Page: not specified (unknown). Give: a link from a site they do not name (fail). Quality: cannot be
  measured for a site that is not named.
- Decision: **Need details** (T5). It is a 3-way exchange, so no page is discussed until all three domains,
  every page, every link direction and any payment are known. If the reply shows the sister site is below
  the floors or the link is paid, Decline with T10.

## Fieldcup Roasters, guest-post swap on a frozen page

- Ask: a small specialty roaster (it sells beans, which Brewnook does not) offers a guest article on its
  blog mentioning Brewnook, at a URL it names, if Brewnook adds a paragraph and link to its "Pour-over
  ratio guide". That guide is under editorial review until next month and is frozen.
- Checks: Who: adjacent business (pass). Want: a contextual mention (pass). Page: frozen (fail). Give: a
  named page with its URL (pass). Quality: traffic, on-topic keywords and referring domains above the
  floors, indexable (pass).
- Decision: **Counter** (T3). Offer two guides Brewnook is updating this month where a mention of a roaster
  fits, and ask which page they would link from and the sentence they suggest.

## Kettlewick, paid placement on the most-cited guide

- Ask: another home coffee-gear shop offers a fee for a "sponsored" link on Brewnook's grinder-settings
  chart, which is Brewnook's most-cited page in AI answers. The email quotes strong traffic figures.
- Checks: Who: sells what Brewnook sells, a direct competitor (fail). Want: a link on our page (fail).
  Page: the most-cited page (fail). Give: money, not a page (fail). Quality: not needed once the first
  check fails.
- Decision: **Decline** (T4). A payment or quoted metrics do not change the answer. Send T4 once; any
  follow-up gets no further reply.
- Takeaway: competitors use AI citation data to find a brand's most-cited pages and target those. The
  more cited the page, the firmer the no.

## The Commute Kitchen, accept

- Ask: a food blog for office workers proposes linking to Brewnook from "Five ways to make better coffee at
  work" on commutekitchen.example, if Brewnook links to that article from its office and travel brewing
  guide, which the team is refreshing this month.
- Checks: Who: a publisher (pass). Want: a contextual mention (pass). Page: being updated (pass). Give: a
  named, live URL (pass). Quality: all measured values above the floors, topic match, indexable, link in
  the main text (pass).
- Decision: **Accept** (T6). Publish once their link is live and checked, then close with T7. Re-check
  their link after 30 days.

## Pitchside Weekly, polite no

- Ask: a fantasy-football news site offers a link from an article about matchday snacks in return for a
  link from Brewnook's blog.
- Checks: Who: not a competitor (pass). Want: a link from our blog (no page fits). Page: none of Brewnook's
  pages covers the topic (fail). Give: a named URL (pass). Quality: on-topic keywords below the floor
  (fail).
- Decision: **Decline** with T10, the polite no for a non-competitor that is not a fit.
